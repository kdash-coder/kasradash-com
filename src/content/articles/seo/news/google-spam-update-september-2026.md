**The Google spam update September 2026 started rolling out on 24 September 2026**, and one day in it is hitting the same four kinds of site the August spam update hit: exact match domains, sites with no defined entity, one article spun across hundreds of locations, and thin location pages. [Kasra Dash](https://kasradash.com/) tracks 13,000 sites with no defined entity and 3,500 exact match domains, and this page is what that data shows so far, with the fixes. By the end you will know whether your site fits one of the four patterns and what to change this week.

The update is still rolling out. Sites in the numbers below may drop further, and a few will recover. I will update this page as the rollout completes.

## What is the Google spam update September 2026?

**The Google spam update September 2026 is a spam-focused update to Google Search that Google announced on 24 September 2026 and is still rolling out.** It follows the August 2026 spam update by roughly three weeks. In the data I track, it targets the same things: exact match domains, sites Google cannot identify as an entity, spun content, and location pages that swap one word.

Everything below is from sites and domains I track, not from Google's announcement. Google's spam updates page is the primary source for the rollout dates: [Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1uZEj6zMhyC1WOiC/history).

## Who got hit: the four patterns

**Four kinds of site were hit in the August 2026 spam update, and the same four are being hit in the September 2026 spam update.**

| Pattern | August 2026 spam update | September 2026 spam update (day one) |
|---|---|---|
| Sites with no defined entity (13,000 tracked) | 65% hit | a further 40% of those hit |
| Exact match domains (3,500 tracked) | 48% hit | a further 25% hit |
| One article spun across locations | hit in both updates | hit in both updates |
| Thin location pages (one word swapped) | hit in both updates | hit in both updates |

Day-one numbers move. Treat the September column as a floor, not a final figure.

### 1. Exact match domains

**Exact match domains lost rankings in both the August and September 2026 spam updates.** In August, 48% of the 3,500 exact match domains I track were hit. On day one of the September update a further 25% were hit.

An exact match domain is a domain that is the keyword: bestplumberleads.co.uk, for example. **An exact match domain makes it harder for Google to assign an entity**, because Google cannot tell whether the name is a brand, a service or a description. When the name is ambiguous, there is nothing for Google to trust when a spam update asks "who is this?".

Exact match domains still work in some cases, and I have ranked them. The point is the trend across two updates in a row, not that every one is dead.

### 2. Sites with no defined entity

**Sites with no defined entity were the biggest loser in the August 2026 spam update: 65% of the 13,000 I track were hit.** On day one of the September update, a further 40% of those sites were hit again.

A defined entity means Google can answer four questions about your business from what it finds across the web. If you fail all four, you are a stranger to Google, and strangers get hit first.

The four checks:

1. **The same business name everywhere.** Site, Google Business Profile, directories, social profiles. One spelling.
2. **One address that matches every listing.** Google Business Profile, the footer of your site, every citation, Facebook and other social profiles. This is the NAP check: name, address, phone number, identical everywhere.
3. **Real photos with real people.** Not stock. Sites that lean on stock photos have looked less trustworthy for four or five years and keep getting hit over time.
4. **Other websites mentioning your brand.** Sites you do not own, saying who you are.

If you have two or three of the four, work on the ones you are missing. If you have none, that is the whole job.

### 3. One article spun across hundreds of locations

**Spun location content was hit in both the August and September 2026 spam updates.** This is one article published hundreds of times with the place name swapped: tree surgeon in Miami, tree surgeon in Orlando, tree surgeon in Tampa, same page each time.

Google treats the set as one thin page repeated, because that is what it is. The fix is not more pages. It is one page per location that says something only true of that location, or no page at all.

### 4. Thin location pages

**Location pages that only swap the location word were hit in both updates.** These are the same problem as pattern three at a smaller scale: a services page copied for each town with nothing local on it.

If a location page has no local address, no local photos, no local reviews and no local reference, it is a spun page with a smaller footprint.

## What is winning instead

**Real businesses with a consistent entity footprint are taking the rankings that exact match domains and rank-and-rent sites lost.** The sites that gained in August, and are gaining on day one of September, have all four entity checks in place: one name, one address everywhere, real photos, and third-party mentions.

This is the same shape as what AI models look for when they decide whether to cite a brand. ## How to audit your site against this update

**Audit your site against the four patterns before the rollout finishes, because a site hit on day one can be hit harder on day five.**

1. **Check the domain.** Is the domain a keyword? If so, you are in the exact match group and the entity work below matters more, not less.
2. **Run the four entity checks.** Name, address, photos, third-party mentions. Write down which ones fail.
3. **Count your location pages.** Open five at random. If you could swap the town name and nothing else would change, they are spun.
4. **Fix the entity first.** Match the name and address everywhere, replace stock photos with real ones, and get mentions on sites you do not own. This is the change that moved sites in August.
5. **Consolidate or rebuild location pages.** Keep the ones with real local content. Merge or remove the rest.
6. **Check Search Console daily until the rollout ends.** Note which pages dropped and on which day, so you can tell the spam update from anything else.

## Common mistakes I see after a spam update

**Buying links to recover.** A spam update that hit you for having no entity does not care how many links you buy. It cares whether Google can identify you.

**Changing the domain in a panic.** Moving an exact match domain to a brand domain is sometimes right, but not on day one of a rollout, and never without fixing the entity checks first.

**Deleting every location page.** Some of them have real local content and real rankings. Audit before you delete.

**Waiting for the rollout to end before doing anything.** The entity fixes take weeks to be picked up. Start now.
