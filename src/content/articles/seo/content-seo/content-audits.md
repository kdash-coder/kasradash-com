## What Is a Content Audit?

**A content audit → is → a systematic review of all website content to assess quality, performance, and relevance.**  
It identifies which pages should be updated, merged, or removed to maintain a healthy and effective site.

**Auditing content → aligns → SEO goals with business objectives.**

### Core Purposes

- Detect underperforming or outdated content.
- Identify duplication and keyword cannibalisation.
- Improve internal linking and topical relevance.
- Refine structure for improved crawlability.

→ Strengthen your site’s depth through [**Topical Authority**](/seo/content-seo/topical-authority/).

Every great content strategy begins with clear visibility over what already exists.

### What is a content audit in SEO?

A content audit in SEO is the process of analysing all your website’s pages to determine which ones are performing well, which need improvement, and which should be removed or consolidated.

## Why Content Audits Matter

### 1. Prevent Content Decay

Over time, rankings decline due to algorithm changes or outdated information.

**Regular audits → prevent → performance drops from ageing content.**

→ Revive lost rankings using [**Optimise Old Content**](/seo/content-seo/content-pruning/).

Neglected content silently drains authority.

### 2. Strengthen Topical Authority

By merging weak posts and linking related ones, audits enhance your topical depth and internal relevance.

**Consolidated content → reinforces → semantic trust.**

→ Learn how this impacts authority in [**Semantic SEO**](/seo/content-seo/topical-authority/).

### 3. Improve User Experience

Eliminating redundant or irrelevant content simplifies navigation, reduces bounce rates, and builds reader trust.

**Content clarity → supports → better engagement metrics.**

Good UX and SEO success are inseparable.

### Why is a content audit important?

A content audit is important because it keeps your website accurate, relevant, and competitive by removing low-quality or outdated pages that harm SEO performance.

## How to Perform a Content Audit (Step-by-Step)

### Step 1: Inventory All Content

Export all URLs using tools like **Screaming Frog**, **Semrush**, or **Ahrefs**. Include metadata, word counts, traffic data, and publish dates.

**Comprehensive inventory → enables → holistic analysis.**

→ Use the template layout below to structure your inventory.

### Step 2: Assess Content Quality

Evaluate each page for accuracy, readability, and intent alignment.

**Content evaluation → highlights → improvement priorities.**

→ Align purpose with [**Search Intent**](/seo/content-seo/search-intent/).

### Step 3: Analyse SEO Performance

Check each page’s rankings, clicks, impressions, and backlinks using **Google Search Console** or **Ahrefs**.

**Performance data → reveals → high-impact optimisation opportunities.**

Measure first; modify second.

### Step 4: Categorise Each Page

Label pages as **Keep**, **Update**, **Merge**, or **Remove** based on performance.

| Status | Action | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Keep** | Performing well | High-traffic evergreen guide |
| **Update** | Refresh and improve | Outdated but relevant post |
| **Merge** | Combine similar topics | Two posts on the same subject |
| **Remove** | Delete or redirect | Thin or duplicate content |

**Categorisation → simplifies → content decision-making.**

→ Learn how to refine with [**Content Pruning**](/seo/content-seo/content-pruning/).

### Step 5: Improve and Update

Enhance structure, add entities, and improve internal linking to support topical clusters.

**Enhancements → elevate → semantic relationships and ranking potential.**

→ Connect updates through [**Internal Linking**](/seo/content-seo/internal-linking/).

### Step 6: Track and Measure Results

Use analytics dashboards to monitor changes after implementation.

**Tracking progress → confirms → optimisation success.**

→ Measure growth via [**Performance Metrics**](/seo/content-seo/).

A well-audited site compounds in value every quarter.

### How do I perform a content audit?

To perform a content audit, collect all URLs, assess performance data, categorise each page, improve what’s valuable, and remove or merge what isn’t.

## What Should a Content Audit Template Include?

**Structure → simplifies → analysis.** A ready-to-use audit template looks like this:

| URL | Title | Primary Keyword | Cluster | Organic Sessions | CTR | Conversion Rate | Content Score | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| /semantic-seo/ | What Is Semantic SEO? | semantic seo | Content SEO | 2,430 | 7.1% | 1.8% | 92 | Keep |
| /ai-seo-tools/ | Best AI Tools for SEO | ai seo tools | AI SEO | 1,950 | 5.9% | 2.3% | 84 | Update |
| /old-blog-post/ | SEO Tips 2021 | seo tips | General | 140 | 0.9% | 0.1% | 42 | Remove |

Include **non-indexed pages** too — they still influence crawl flow and internal linking.

### What makes a good content score metric?

Combine performance and quality factors, for example:
`(Traffic x Engagement Rate x Entity Density) / (Bounce Rate + Age Factor)`
Normalise it on a 100-point scale for easy comparison.

### How deep should internal-link checks go?

Flag orphaned pages, broken links, and crawl depth — important pages should sit **≤3 clicks from the homepage**, and **3–10 contextual internal links per 1,000 words** typically balances readability and crawl efficiency.

Screaming Frog, Google Search Console, and Ahrefs are the best tools because they reveal technical, traffic, and backlink data that define performance quality.

## Turning Audit Insights into Action

Audits only add value when their results feed into structured content planning and execution.

### 1. Update and Republish

Revise outdated data, improve formatting, and add entities for stronger semantic relevance.

**Freshness → signals → relevance to Google.**

→ Combine this process with **Optimise Old Content**.

### 2. Merge Related Topics

Combine posts that cover similar ideas to strengthen depth and reduce cannibalisation.

**Merging → consolidates → authority and engagement.**

### 3. Remove or Redirect Weak Content

If a page adds no value or traffic, delete it or redirect to a stronger, related page.

**Pruning → improves → crawl efficiency and user focus.**

→ Implement this safely with **Content Pruning**.

### 4. Add Missing Internal Links

Use anchor text to guide users and crawlers through related topics.

**Linking → reinforces → topical hierarchy.**

→ Plan this through the [**Content Frameworks**](/seo/content-seo/content-frameworks/).

### 5. Feed Findings into a Content Calendar

Schedule updates and new article creation based on audit insights.

**Systematic scheduling → turns → audits into continuous improvement.**

→ Maintain consistency using the [**Content Calendar**](/seo/content-seo/).

Action beats analysis when it comes to SEO growth.

### What should I do after a content audit?

After a content audit, update or merge outdated pages, remove thin content, add internal links, and feed insights into a future content calendar.

## Common Content Audit Mistakes

1. **Deleting Without Redirecting:** Breaks link equity and confuses crawlers.
2. **Ignoring Internal Links:** Leads to orphaned content.
3. **Skipping Metadata Updates:** Titles and descriptions must match revised topics.
4. **Not Measuring Results:** Fails to validate improvement impact.

**Avoidance of errors → preserves → content value and authority.**

The wrong audit can hurt as much as a bad post.

### What are common mistakes in content audits?

Deleting pages without redirects, ignoring internal links, and skipping follow-up tracking are common mistakes that reduce SEO effectiveness.
